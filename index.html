Step 1 · every time you pick it up

Tune up first

An out-of-tune guitar makes even perfect fingers sound wrong. Let the tuner listen while you pluck one string at a time, or tap a peg to hear how that string should sound.

Tune up to the note

If a string is too high, loosen it until it is a little low, then tighten back up. The string holds its tune better that way.

Small turns, big change

Turn the peg a tiny bit, pluck again, and watch the needle. Strings can snap if you crank them too tight.

    How to read a chord picture
    • Up-and-down lines are the strings. The thickest string is on the left (on the right in Lefty mode).
    • Side-to-side lines are the frets. The thick bar at the top is the nut, the end of the neck.
    • Coloured dots show where to press, and the number tells you which finger.
    • O above a string: play it open, with no finger. X: don’t play that string.
    • Press with your fingertip, just behind the fret wire, not on top of it.
